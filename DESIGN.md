# Seams Familiar design

## North Star

An orderly workwear catalog with the personality of instant prints and hand-marked shop tags. Put the garment, fit, price, and shop in view before asking the shopper to open details.

## Color and type

The Scout stylesheet owns the runtime palette. Warm stone (#EBE8E1) sits behind the public catalog’s eight photographic instant-print frames. The masthead uses soft white (#FFFEFA) and a self-hosted Fraunces Semibold wordmark. Plum ink and violet controls define the browsing surface. Frames shuffle across each catalog page without repetition in a row. Each aperture contains the seller photo, with spacing below for the price tag. The 80s view adds a vertical multicolor film stripe. The 90s view uses an uninterrupted black-and-white checkerboard along the left edge and the purple-and-teal zigzag on the right. Use the existing Inter and system-sans stack for product titles, prices, sizes, and controls.

## Layout and interaction

Keep a two-column photo grid on phones and four columns at desktop width. Align the frames and give the 80s prints a deeper lower border. Keep original seller photos fully visible inside the frame. Use the existing Resonate logo in the header. Give the shop its own line beneath the size, with a small storefront icon.

Decade links filter the catalog by seller date clues and preserve the selected decade when searching or switching shelves. All decades leaves room for substantial 2000s pieces that meet the catalog’s quality criteria. Other collection controls refine the results. Local pickup is an optional, collapsed search that uses a ZIP only after submission. Seller names appear on cards without pickup addresses. The Goodwill pants shelf uses shippable listings and links to ShopGoodwill’s own search for its separate inventory. A card opens the listing detail. The original seller link sits beside the price and size, before the photo gallery and detailed evidence on mobile. The paper price tag is the playful motion detail. It swings once on pointer hover or keyboard focus and stays still with reduced motion. First Dibs explains the number of picks, matching criteria, and what each email contains before a shopper opts in from their account. Fresh finds opens with live listings and guest filters. Email verification is available when a shopper chooses to save sizing and preferences.


## Review after each change

1. Name the shopper task and its success measure before building. For a visual-only change, use a real garment photo and the same card content for comparison.
2. Build the smallest version. Review a phone and a desktop view with mixed seller photography and fully loaded assets.
3. Look for three things. Does the shop feel authentic, can someone compare and act easily, and does the change help a measurable shopper or partner outcome?
4. Fix the highest-impact issue and repeat the same task with the same examples. Check the whole path through filters, listing detail, seller link, and return to results when touched by the change.
5. Report the result, the biggest remaining tradeoff, and one concrete next improvement. Distinguish seller clicks from merchant-confirmed orders. Confirm value to partners before adding sponsored placement.
